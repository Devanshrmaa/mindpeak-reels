  <p>Kinematics is the gateway chapter to all of JEE Mechanics and typically appears in 2 to 3 JEE Main questions per paper. The 7-step strategy below solves 95 percent of standard kinematics problems.</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Draw a clear diagram</strong> — mark position, velocity, acceleration directions explicitly.</li>
    <li><strong>Choose a coordinate system upfront</strong> — pick axes that simplify vector decomposition.</li>
    <li><strong>List knowns and unknowns</strong> — explicitly write them on paper, not mentally.</li>
    <li><strong>Identify the type</strong> — uniform acceleration, projectile, relative motion, or non-uniform.</li>
    <li><strong>Apply the right kinematic equation</strong> — most problems use v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², or v² = u² + 2as.</li>
    <li><strong>Check units and signs</strong> — sign errors cause 60 percent of kinematics losses in our student data.</li>
    <li><strong>Verify with a special case</strong> — set one variable to zero and check the answer reduces correctly.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>In our one-on-one Mechanics classes, students who follow this 7-step method on paper (rather than jumping to formulas) cut their kinematics error rate in half within 4 weeks.</p>
