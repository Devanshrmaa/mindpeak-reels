<p>The strategy that separates 95+ percentile from 99+ percentile in Kinematics is treating every problem as a sketch-first, equation-second exercise. Roughly 7–9 JEE Main marks come from Kinematics annually, and another 4–8 in JEE Advanced. The seven steps below close 80% of kinematics mistakes.</p>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Sketch the motion before reading equations</strong>. Mark initial and final positions, velocity vectors, and reference frame on paper. 60% of kinematics mistakes are visualization errors, not algebra errors.</li>
  <li><strong>Pick reference frame consciously</strong>. Ground frame for projectile, observer frame for relative velocity, rotating frame only for advanced cases. Frame mix-ups cost JEE Advanced marks routinely.</li>
  <li><strong>Decompose 2D motion into independent axes</strong>. Horizontal and vertical components evolve independently — never mix them in a single equation.</li>
  <li><strong>Use kinematic equations only after free-body / motion sketch</strong>. v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as — pick the equation by what's known and what's asked, not by habit.</li>
  <li><strong>Relative velocity: always state "of A with respect to B"</strong>. JEE Advanced kinematics regularly punishes students who skip the "with respect to" framing.</li>
  <li><strong>Boundary conditions are exam-day gold</strong>. Particle at rest at t=0? Particle on the ground at maximum height? State boundary conditions explicitly, even if obvious.</li>
  <li><strong>Cross-check via dimensional analysis</strong>. Every final answer should pass a dimensional check before bubbling. 30 seconds of dimensional check has saved 4 marks per mock for our students.</li>
</ol>

<p>In our 1-on-1 sessions, <strong>Devansh (IIT-BHU)</strong> teaches kinematics to <strong>every dropper batch first</strong> because the visualization habit it builds carries into Mechanics, Electrodynamics, and Modern Physics. <h2>NEET Physics Numericals — 8 Tactics That Add 30 Marks</h2>

<p>Physics numericals are where NEET students lose the most marks — not from concept gaps, but from calculation slips and time pressure. The 8 tactics below add 25–35 marks across a 180-mark Physics paper for our 1-on-1 students who internalize them.</p>

  <li><strong>One-step shortcuts via dimensional analysis</strong>. Several NEET Physics MCQs are solvable by dimensional analysis alone, in 30 seconds.</li>
  <li><strong>Skip-and-return discipline</strong>. Any numerical taking more than 90 seconds — mark, skip, return at the end. Saves 4–6 marks worth of bonus questions.</li>
